# Henn-na Hotel

**Domain:** Hospitality
**Source:** Bhimasta and Kuo (2019)

## Background

The Henn-na Hotel in Japan adopted service robots at scale for
guest-facing tasks. The deployment was later substantially
reconfigured, with many robots withdrawn.

## Evidence

- Analysis of 250 negative guest reviews across four booking platforms.
- Problems identified: perceived usefulness, ease of use, human
  intervention, embodiment, expectation gaps, service efficiency.

## SLMO encoding

    <sustained operation,
     human / technical / workflow,
     capability-expectation mismatch + usefulness + ease of use,
     human intervention / degraded service>

## Operational lesson

Technically available automation can still produce poor service when a
robot's capabilities do not match what guests expect from the assigned
role. When guests repeatedly require staff assistance, automation
shifts work rather than eliminating it.

For practitioners: evaluate whether the resulting human–robot service
is better, easier, or more sustainable than the workflow it replaces.

## Source

Bhimasta, R. A., and Kuo, P.-Y. *What causes the adoption failure of
