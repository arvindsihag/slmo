# Robo-Barista

**Domain:** Service / residential
**Source:** Wilson et al., *Faulty Coffees: Barriers to Adoption of an
In-the-wild Robo-Barista* (2026)

## Background

A robotic barista was deployed for five weeks in an over-50s housing
complex. Residents could obtain a free daily coffee through
interaction with the robot.

## Evidence

- Initial curiosity was high.
- Repeat interaction remained low.
- The study documented technical breakdowns, accessibility barriers,
  curiosity without retention, and social dynamics specific to the
  residential setting.

## SLMO encoding

    <sustained operation,
     human / technical / social context,
     accessibility + reliability + retention,
     reduced repeat use>

## Operational lesson

Initial engagement should not be confused with sustained adoption. A
service concept can attract first-time interaction yet fail to become
part of everyday practice when reliability, accessibility, perceived
value, and local social context are not jointly supported.

For practitioners: separate early engagement metrics from indicators
of repeated voluntary use.
