# Blue Jay

**Domain:** Logistics
**Sources:** Amazon (2025, 2026); TechCrunch (2026); Business Insider (2026)

## Background

Amazon introduced Blue Jay, a multi-arm robotics system, in October
2025. It had moved from concept to production in just over a year and
was being tested in a South Carolina facility, where it could pick,
stow, and consolidate approximately 75% of the item types handled at
the site.

In February 2026, Amazon stated that Blue Jay was no longer being used
in operations, while emphasising that its underlying technologies
would continue to support other robotics programmes.

## Evidence

- Production-tested prototype introduced October 2025.
- Discontinuation in operations confirmed by Amazon, February 2026.
- External reporting attributed the halt to high cost, manufacturing
  complexity, and implementation difficulties.

## SLMO encoding

    <pilot / scaling,
     organisational / economic / technical,
     cost + manufacturing + integration,
     discontinuation in operations>

## Operational lesson

Blue Jay is not a conventional HRI field failure, nor is it evidence
that the underlying robotics technology was unsuccessful. It
illustrates a distinct deployment outcome: promising technical
capability may still fail to justify continued operationalisation when
manufacturing, integration, cost, and scaling constraints become
dominant.

For practitioners: distinguish technical capability from
pilot-to-scale viability.
